# PassportPilot — Feasibility Investigation (Refresh, 15 July 2026)
*Lead Venture Architect assessment. Claims tagged: [empirically established] / [plausible] / [founder optimism — unverified]. Vendor self-interested data flagged inline.*

## TL;DR
- **NO-GO as currently framed; conditional GO on a re-pivot to a GPSR-first wedge.** The pitch's "why now" is materially weaker than claimed: the 19 July 2026 DPP registry deadline is an *infrastructure obligation on the European Commission, not a seller obligation*; no product-specific ESPR delegated act has yet entered into force; the first mandatory DPP (batteries) is 18 February 2027; and textiles realistically ~2028/2029. GPSR fines are national and cap near €100,000 in the largest markets — **not** the "€10M / 4% of turnover" headline. The genuinely live wedge is GPSR technical-file + Responsible-Person automation, not DPP.
- **The platform kill-switch is already blinking.** Amazon and Etsy have shipped free native GPSR compliance fields; Amazon discussed DPPs explicitly at its inaugural Safety Day in Munich (May 2026), and AWS has published an agentic-AI-for-DPP reference architecture on Bedrock AgentCore. Real competitors (Marqetir, Fluxy.One at €1/passport, eugpsr.eu, EaseCert, Euverify, Eldris) already occupy the space at low prices. The intended moat is thin and decaying.
- **Validate before building.** Three zero-code tests (fake-door LOI test, confirm no Amazon native full-DPP automation, verify the true in-scope category timeline) with numeric thresholds should gate any code. Two facts hold up cleanly and de-risk the legal side: the tool is **not** EU-AI-Act high-risk, and B2B SaaS sales to Chinese firms are **outside EU VAT scope**.

## Key Findings

**1. The regulatory clock is real but mis-scoped.** [empirically established] ESPR (Regulation (EU) 2024/1781) Article 13(1) requires the European Commission to *set up* the central DPP registry by 19 July 2026 — a Commission infrastructure deadline, not a seller registration duty. As DPPA AS (a member of Standard Norge's DPP committee SN/K 624, the Norwegian mirror of CEN-CLC/JTC 24) puts it: "The Commission must set up the registry by 19 July 2026. That is an infrastructure deadline on the Commission, not a date by which manufacturers must register their products." The registry is a federated *index* (unique identifier + pointer to a passport hosted by the operator or its provider), not a central data store.

**2. The registry implementing regulation is not confirmed adopted as of mid-July 2026.** [plausible] The Commission published a draft implementing regulation on 29 April 2026 (Ref. Ares(2026)4424976; WTO notification G/TBT/N/EU/1211) and closed public consultation on 27 May 2026. As of 15 July 2026, no primary EUR-Lex/Official Journal source confirms formal adoption; secondary vendor/law-firm sources describe adoption as "expected" in the June–July 2026 window, with go-live engineered ahead of the February 2027 battery obligation. Traceable Digital, "last verified 7 July 2026," reported "no Commission announcement of delay to the Article 13 set-up deadline has surfaced." In late May 2026, CEN and CENELEC published the first six core European standards for the DPP (identifiers, data carriers, data exchange, storage, APIs, interoperability) — a supporting infrastructure milestone, not a seller obligation.

**3. No product-specific DPP is yet mandatory.** [empirically established] No ESPR delegated act has entered into force. Batteries come first: Article 77 of the EU Battery Regulation (2023/1542) makes the battery passport apply "from 18 February 2027 to each LMT battery, each industrial battery with a capacity above 2 kWh, and each electric vehicle battery placed on the market or put into service." The textiles delegated act is expected ~Q2 2027 (PassportCraft co-founder Irina Aguiar) with an ~18-month transition, pushing mandatory textile compliance to ~late 2028/2029. Iron & steel adoption ~2026; electronics/ICT later (2028–2030). The destruction ban on unsold clothing/footwear for large enterprises applies from 19 July 2026 — but that is not a DPP obligation and does not touch small Chinese sellers.

**4. GPSR is the live wedge, not DPP.** [empirically established] GPSR (Regulation (EU) 2023/988) has applied since 13 December 2024. It mandates an EU Responsible Person (Art. 16), technical documentation, traceability, and listing-level safety information. Amazon has enforced since December 2024 via its "Manage Your Compliance" / Regulatory Compliance dashboard, suppressing ASINs that lack a valid manufacturer + EU Responsible Person + safety warnings. This is a genuine, present pain point for Chinese sellers — the opposite of the DPP, which is a future one.

**5. Penalties are national and far below the pitch's headline.** [empirically established] GPSR sets no fines itself (Art. 44 requires "effective, proportionate and dissuasive" national penalties). Germany's revised ProdSG (published in the Federal Law Gazette 5 February 2026; in force 19 February 2026) caps fines at €100,000 for the most serious offences and €10,000 for lesser ones, plus profit-skimming under the OWiG, with §29 ProdSG adding imprisonment up to one year for repeat or endangering offences (Baker McKenzie, March 2026). Italy's Legislative Decree No. 78/2026 (Official Gazette No. 111, 15 May 2026; in force 16 May 2026) sets, under new Art. 112(1) of the Consumer Code, "imprisonment from six months to one year and a fine from EUR 10,000 to EUR 100,000, increased up to EUR 150,000 where the product presents a serious risk." Austria applies penalties via its product-safety/administrative-penal framework in a similar order of magnitude. **The "€10M / 4% of turnover" figure repeated by some vendors (e.g., Marqetir's and 24hour-AR's guide pages) conflates GPSR with other EU regimes and is not accurate for GPSR seller fines — flag as self-interested vendor spin.**

**6. Platform-risk kill scenario is escalating.** [plausible] Amazon hosted its inaugural Safety Day in Munich in May 2026 (attended by "nine regulatory bodies from seven countries" and brands including Hasbro, Mattel, Ravensburger and Stihl; opened by DACH Country Manager Rocco Braeuniger: "Product safety is not something any one of us can solve alone"). Amazon stated: "For selling partners, DPPs could reduce administrative burden and operational complexity. With ambitious DPP legislation, small and medium-sized businesses may be able to make all their product packaging updates with just one click." AWS's "for Industries" blog published an agentic-AI-for-DPP reference architecture on Amazon Bedrock AgentCore and cites that "according to research commissioned by Amazon and conducted by Oxera, DPPs have the potential to transform product information sharing." **No native full-DPP seller-automation product has shipped yet** — but native GPSR fields are already free on Amazon and Etsy, and the intent signals point directly at the founder's proposed territory.

**7. Competitors are numerous, credible, and cheap.** [empirically established, vendor-reported pricing] The Responsible-Person / GPSR market clusters at €195–€549/yr: eugpsr.eu (€199–549/yr), Euverify (from €200/yr), Eldris (£195 one-time + £19.95/mo), EaseCert (€400 for clothing/accessories, €500 for electronics/toys, one-time), 24hour-AR (~€1,650/yr for ≤20 products). On the DPP side, Fluxy.One advertises "from €1 per DPP" (a 500-pack for €500) with AI document parsing and GS1 QR generation. **Marqetir has repriced since the prior assessment: its live pricing page now shows a "Founders Program" with Starter at €99/mo, Growth (Most Popular) at a promotional €99/mo (normally €249/mo, up from the previously assessed €179/mo), and a "Done For You" managed tier from €3,000/mo** — the prior €59/€179 figures no longer appear. Etsy has shipped native product-safety fields and partnered with Cert-Rep and International Associates for RP services; Webinterpret bundles GPSR into eBay international plans.

**8. The tool is not high-risk under the EU AI Act, and VAT is clean.** [empirically established] A compliance-document-generation SaaS falls in none of the eight Annex III high-risk categories (biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment, essential services, law enforcement, migration, justice) and performs no profiling of natural persons, so it is not high-risk under Article 6. A documented Article 6(4) non-high-risk self-assessment is prudent housekeeping but triggers no conformity-assessment burden. B2B electronically-supplied services from an Austrian founder to Chinese business customers are outside the scope of EU VAT — under Article 44 of the VAT Directive the place of supply is the customer's country, so no EU VAT is due (input VAT on related expenses generally remains deductible).

## Details

### Module 1 — Market Viability & Go-to-Market
The desperate buyer today is **not** a DPP buyer — it is a **GPSR-exposed Chinese Amazon/Etsy seller facing listing suppression.** Amazon's enforcement since December 2024 delists ASINs lacking a valid EU Responsible Person; that is the acute, present pain. Hardest near-term exposure sits with toys, electronics, and personal-care goods (higher scrutiny categories), though GPSR covers virtually all non-food consumer products. DPP-driven urgency does not reach textiles until ~2028 and electronics later, so a DPP-first pitch is selling a 2028 problem in 2026.

Willingness-to-pay is anchored low by incumbents (RP services €195–€549/yr; Fluxy.One passports from €1; EaseCert €400–500 one-time). A solo EU founder with no China presence realistically **cannot** reach the first five paying Chinese sellers unaided: a reseller/partner channel — Shenzhen sourcing agencies, freight forwarders, WeChat/1688 group operators, or a PingPong onboarding partner (whose default desk operates in Mandarin) — is effectively **mandatory**. Payment rails are not a barrier: PingPong (~1%, ~0.6% at volume), WorldFirst (~0.3% FX, Ant-owned, deepest 1688 integration), and Payoneer (up to ~1–3.99% depending on method) all support USD SaaS collection and RMB payout.

### Module 2 — Solo & AI Execution Architecture
The founder's stack maps well to the *actual* (GPSR) problem: RAG over GPSR / national-transposition (ProdSG, Italian Consumer Code) / penalty corpora; a document-generation pipeline for technical files, risk assessments, and Declarations of Conformity; a GS1 Digital Link data-carrier generator (open standard — `https://domain/01/{GTIN}`, built on ISO/IEC 15459 for identification and ISO/IEC 18004 for QR) for forward DPP readiness; multi-marketplace field mapping (Amazon, Etsy, Allegro, Kaufland, eBay, bol.com each differ); and a Chinese-language UX. Maintenance is the hidden cost: national penalty regimes and delegated acts change often (Germany Feb 2026, Italy May 2026, CEN/CENELEC standards May 2026, registry implementing act mid-2026). Automate via scheduled RAG re-indexing of EUR-Lex and Commission pages, with the founder verifying at the spec/decision layer — a good fit for his multi-agent orchestration strengths.

### Module 3 — Scientific Moat & Investor Appeal
Blunt: the moat is thin and decays. Regulatory know-how erodes as rules stabilize and as marketplaces absorb the function for free. What can compound: (a) an accumulating structured product-attribute dataset keyed to GTINs (weak network effect); (b) compliance-data switching costs once a seller's full catalog lives in the tool; (c) a Responsible-Person partner network the founder brokers. The single investor-moving metric is **pre-pay conversion / signed LOIs from real Chinese sellers**, because that de-risks the true bottleneck — distribution access despite no China presence — rather than the (already-proven) technical feasibility. "Why now" is real for GPSR but decaying; it is **not** yet real for DPP.

### Module 4 — Reality-Check Hurdles
- **Platform kill scenario:** medium-high likelihood over 18–36 months. Amazon's Safety Day DPP discussion and AWS's agentic-DPP architecture are leading indicators. If Amazon ships native full-DPP automation, the DPP wedge collapses; the GPSR-document-generation + RP-brokerage layer is somewhat more durable because it spans marketplaces and off-Amazon channels.
- **Legal exposure of generating documents:** defensible if positioned as software tooling with the seller as the attesting economic operator; marketing "certification" the founder isn't performing would create liability.
- **EU Responsible Person liability:** real and ongoing (holding technical docs, authority liaison, corrective action). Broker/partner this role; never provide it personally.
- **DPP go-live scope on 19 July 2026:** infrastructure only; **no** seller category in-scope that day.
- **EU AI Act:** not high-risk (Article 6 / Annex III); document a self-assessment.
- **Cross-border VAT:** B2B to Chinese businesses is outside EU VAT scope; report as out-of-scope on the Austrian return and keep evidence of the customer's non-EU business status.

### Module 5 — Timeline & Budget
Naive estimate: a compliance-document workflow demo in ~4 weekends. Applying a 2–4× optimism-bias correction for a solo founder at 8–15 hrs/week with a demanding full-time job and a 3.5-year-old: **realistically 8–14 calendar weeks** to a credible investor demo (call it ~10–20 effective working weekends).

Itemized budget to a functional PoC (≤$1,000): domain ~$15; Supabase/Railway/Fly.io free-to-$25/mo (~$75 over 3 months); Claude Max already paid ($0 marginal); incidental API/testing ~$100; GS1 Digital Link tooling on free tiers ($0); landing-page + cold-email tooling ~$50; buffer ~$150. **Total ≈ $390, comfortably under $1,000.** The $5k stretch is **not** justified before the validation gate is passed.

### Module 6 — Go/No-Go & Validation Tests
**Decision: NO-GO on the DPP-first framing; conditional GO on a GPSR-first re-pivot, gated by three zero-code tests before any code is written:**
1. **Fake-door LOI test.** Cold-approach 50 EU-importing Chinese sellers (via a partner / WeChat / Amazon-seller forums) with a "GPSR-compliant technical file + DoC + EU Responsible Person in 24h" offer. **Threshold: ≥10% (5 sellers) give a signed LOI or pre-pay intent.** Below 10% → NO-GO.
2. **Platform-risk check.** Confirm Amazon has NOT announced native full-DPP automation (watch Accelerate, Sept 22–24 2026). **Threshold: if announced, drop the DPP wedge and reassess GPSR-only viability.**
3. **Scope verification.** Confirm the true in-scope timeline (currently: nothing seller-facing on 19 July 2026; batteries 18 Feb 2027; textiles ~2028). **Threshold: proceed only if all external messaging matches the GPSR-now / DPP-later reality — no manufactured DPP urgency.**

## Recommendations
1. **Re-pivot the wedge to GPSR now, DPP later.** Lead with GPSR technical-file + DoC generation + RP brokerage — a live 2026 pain with real delisting consequences. Treat DPP as a 2027–2028 roadmap upsell, timed to the battery (Feb 2027) and textile (~2028) obligations.
2. **Secure a distribution partner before writing code.** The binding constraint is China reach, not engineering. Run the fake-door test *through* a partner; a ≥10% intent rate is the gate to any build.
3. **Broker, never provide, the Responsible Person role.** Partner with an existing RP provider (there are many at €195–€549/yr) and compete on automation and speed, not on being the liable EU entity.
4. **Position as tooling, not certification.** Keep the attesting economic operator as the seller; document an EU-AI-Act non-high-risk self-assessment; report B2B China sales as out-of-scope for VAT.
5. **Cap spend at ≤$1,000 and plan for 8–14 weeks.** Do not scale toward the $5k stretch until the LOI threshold is met.
6. **Benchmarks that change the plan:** Amazon ships native full-DPP automation → drop DPP entirely; a textiles delegated act is adopted with a <18-month transition → accelerate DPP; LOI intent rate <10% → NO-GO; a competitor (e.g., Marqetir or Fluxy.One) ships agentic GPSR technical-file generation with China distribution → reassess differentiation immediately.

## What Changed Since the Prior Assessment
- **Registry:** The prior read (Art. 13(1) = Commission infrastructure deadline, not a seller obligation) is **confirmed and reinforced.** New since mid-2026: the draft implementing regulation was published 29 April 2026 (Ares(2026)4424976; WTO G/TBT/N/EU/1211), consultation closed 27 May 2026, and CEN/CENELEC published the first six core DPP standards in late May 2026. Formal adoption/OJ publication was **not** confirmable as of 15 July 2026 — treat as "expected, not done."
- **Timelines:** Battery passport 18 Feb 2027 and textiles ~2028/2029 both **confirmed unchanged.** No acceleration; the 2025 EU Omnibus left ESPR/DPP obligations untouched.
- **GPSR penalties:** Now concretely fixed by two new national laws that post-date the prior assessment — Germany's revised ProdSG (in force 19 Feb 2026, max €100,000) and Italy's Legislative Decree 78/2026 (in force 16 May 2026, €10,000–€150,000 + imprisonment). This further **debunks** the "€10M/4%" claim.
- **Amazon/Etsy tooling:** Native GPSR fields remain free and enforcement has *escalated* into labelling and safety-info checks in 2026. New signal: Amazon's inaugural Safety Day (Munich, May 2026) explicitly floated DPP "one-click" packaging updates, and AWS published an agentic-DPP reference architecture — the platform-risk thesis is now stronger, not weaker. Still **no** shipped native full-DPP seller product.
- **Competitor pricing:** **Marqetir has repriced upward/restructured** — now a Founders Program (Starter €99/mo; Growth promo €99/mo vs. €249/mo normal, up from the prior €179/mo; Done-For-You from €3,000/mo). - **Payment rails / VAT / AI Act:** All **confirmed stable.** PingPong ~1%/WorldFirst ~0.3%/Payoneer functional; B2B-to-China outside EU VAT scope; tool not EU-AI-Act high-risk.

## Caveats
- Registry adoption status relies on vendor/law-firm secondary sources; **primary EUR-Lex/Official Journal confirmation was not obtained** and should be verified directly before any go-to-market messaging references a "live registry."
- Competitor pricing is self-reported vendor data and changes frequently (Marqetir's mid-2026 repricing is a live example); treat all figures as indicative and re-check at fundraising.
- Many DPP-vendor sources have a commercial incentive to overstate urgency; their "deadline" and "€10M/4%" framing has been discounted here in favour of primary regulation text and national penalty laws.
- The feasibility verdict assumes the founder can secure a China distribution partner; absent that, even the GPSR-first pivot is materially weaker, and the honest recommendation trends back toward NO-GO.
