## 1. Terms every approach must state (PREREG-F §2.2, §5.1, §6.2; proposed, card D-002)

These come from PREREG-F, which is still *proposed*. If D-002 revises them, this section
follows.

- **Introduce only.** A partner introduces sellers 1:1 and shares its own channel-coded form
  link (e.g. `?ch=P1`). The seller fills the form or sends the LOI **to the founder
  directly**. The partner never receives form data, contact details or LOIs. It may tell
  the founder *how many* introductions it made (a count, no names). This goes into the CH.2
  agreement.
- **Pay never depends on the outcome.** No per-sign-up or per-LOI pay, and no revenue share
  that exists only after a GO. If a partner asks for money, the only form allowed is a
  **fixed fee** for a bounded scope, and that is a cost card for the founder (hard rule 6).
- **No post-gate promise during the test.** A commercial deal (referral, revenue share,
  reselling, RP brokerage) is negotiated **only after G1**. The founder should not promise
  one, or hint that one depends on the result, while the test runs. Results report signals
  per partner and D recomputed without the partner with the most (PREREG-F §4).
- **Honest framing.** The product is in development. Early access, no payment, the seller
  attests, and PassportPilot certifies nothing and is not the EU Responsible Person (hard
  rules 3 and 5). A partner who already sells GPSR services should hear plainly that the
  tool overlaps with them (see the "Overlap" notes).

**What a partner can honestly gain during the test:** an early look at a tool its clients
ask about; a say in what the product covers (categories, languages, export format); a free
GPSR explainer or webinar for its clients if the founder offers one (content only, no product
claims beyond PREREG-F §1.1; it is public-facing, so every regulatory statement needs a
primary source, it passes `/claims-audit` and goes on a comms card, and any paid webinar tool
is a cost card); and the aggregate result of the test, which tells it whether
the segment wants this. Any expectation of future business is a residual bias. The
per-partner reporting above is the mitigation. The pitch (FD.4) should say plainly that no
deal is promised and that results are reported per partner.

## 6. Profile: a Chinese-speaking BD freelancer or partner

**What they would do in the test.** Choose sellers in their network and introduce them
1:1. Adapt the founder-approved Chinese messages (FD.4) to their voice. Every adapted text goes
back to the founder for approval before use, and the price, the scope and the
in-development wording never change.
Answer "what is this?" in Chinese. Share their own channel-coded form link. Report how many
introductions they made, as a count only. They hold no seller data and collect no LOIs
(§1). They can also check the zh-CN copy, which PREREG-F §5.4 lists as a free alternative to
paid native-speaker review.

**Who fits.** The usual job titles are commercial BD (商务拓展/BD), channel manager
(渠道经理) and channel BD manager (渠道BD经理). The duties are finding partners and
building agent channels ([Liepin](https://www.liepin.com/jobsqudaobdjingli/);
[51job](https://baike.51job.com/zhiwei/02071/)) [plausible, job boards]. Likely backgrounds, all
[plausible] (no source describes a typical CV):
- BD or sales staff at SPN-listed VAT, EPR and EU-rep agencies (tier 1);
- former Amazon Global Selling account managers (the team exists:
  [gs.amazon.cn](https://gs.amazon.cn/strategicseller) [established]);
- cross-border community operators (社群运营).

**Where to look (channel types; the founder searches, the loop does not).**
- Staff introduced by a tier-1 or tier-2 organisation. This is the most credible route.
- Upwork and LinkedIn (any paid posting, platform fee or recruiter tier is a cost card).
  Upwork's China categories lean towards sourcing, not seller-side BD
  ([Upwork CN](https://www.upwork.com/hire/sourcing-freelancers/cn/)) [vendor claim].
- BOSS直聘 and 猎聘 have part-time (兼职) cross-border listings
  ([BOSS example](https://www.zhipin.com/zhaopin/f7c849e4fcedbaad1nV43ti7GA~~/))
  [plausible, a job board]. An employer account may need a mainland phone number, which is not checked.
- Cifnews and 知无不言 service boards (§3).
- No evidence was found for Chinese-student or alumni networks in the EU, or for the
  Canton Fair or Global Sources as recruiting venues.

**Typical arrangements in the market.** None of these numbers is specific to compliance
referrals, and none is a quote.

**What to offer in the test (PREREG-F §5.1).** The market default is commission or revenue
share, and **neither is allowed in the test.** Both pay on outcome, and outcome pay would
bias the count of signals D rests on (PREREG-F §5.1; §8, "Partner's own clients"). Two options:
- **(a) Unpaid co-run (recommended).** Best with a tier-1 to tier-3 organisation, whose staff
  gain §1's benefits as part of their job. It costs nothing.
- **(b) A fixed fee for a bounded scope**, e.g. a set number of hours of copy review and
  introductions, paid whatever the result. This is a **cost card**. Illustrative arithmetic
  from the Upwork rates above, not a quote: 20 hours × US$12–35 (China-based) ≈ US$240–700;
  20 hours × US$30–65 (BD representatives) ≈ US$600–1,300.

Revenue share, referral commission and white-label terms are negotiated only after G1.

**Risks to know before contracting.**
- **Pay-on-outcome pressure.** Most freelance BD people expect commission. Expect to explain
  why the test cannot pay one.
- **Paying an individual in the PRC is not straightforward.**
  - EOR vendors say "independent contractor" is not a recognised category in China, so a
    contract can be treated as de facto employment
    ([Rippling](https://www.rippling.com/blog/worker-classification-in-china)) [vendor claim].
  - Income for services performed in China is taxed in China. With no withholding agent,
    the individual self-declares
    ([chinatax](https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfgk/c100009/c5193028/content.html))
    [established, snippet-read].
  - Contracting a company (one of the organisations above) likely avoids most of this
    [plausible, not checked].
  - Any legal review is a cost card.
- **Seller distrust.** Cross-border training and cheap EU-rep scams are documented
  ([21jingji, 2024-06-24](https://www.21jingji.com/article/20240624/herald/d754f32b387ef3a89db39332671193ba.html)
  [plausible, news]; [Cifnews 172825](https://www.cifnews.com/article/172825) [vendor claim]).
  A known intermediary's name is what gets a reply, so unsolicited messages from an unknown
  freelancer may convert poorly.
- **Paid groups.** WeChat groups and KOL communities often charge for access
  ([woshipm](https://www.woshipm.com/marketing/4281381.html)) [plausible, anecdotal]. A paid
  post is a cost card and is counted as its own channel.
